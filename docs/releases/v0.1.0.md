# Ora 0.1.0: a private voice assistant for your desktop

Ora is a voice assistant for Windows. You talk, it thinks, and it talks back. Speech recognition and speech synthesis both run on your own GPU, so your audio never leaves your computer, and you choose which model does the thinking.

This is the first public release. It's early, so please read "Known limitations" below.

## Highlights

- **Private by design.** Hearing (Whisper) and speaking (Supertonic 3) run locally on WebGPU. Only the text of your requests goes to the model provider you pick.
- **Bring your own brain.** OpenRouter, OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, or your own server such as Ollama or LM Studio (which can make the whole app work offline). Each provider has its own encrypted key and a live model list.
- **Feels like a conversation.** Replies are spoken sentence by sentence as they are written, and you can talk over Ora to interrupt it. Use hands-free listening or push-to-talk.
- **It does things.** An agent with 16 tools (15 on by default): weather, web search, Wikipedia, calculator, timers, web pages, files in a folder you choose, clipboard, and long-term memory. Sensitive actions such as writing files or reading the clipboard ask for your approval first.
- **Made to be tuned.** Ten voices in 31 languages, four Whisper sizes, light and dark themes with five accent colours, a global hotkey (Ctrl+Shift+Space), live captions, and a latency readout under every reply.

## Requirements

- Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit)
- A GPU with WebGPU support (any recent Intel, AMD or NVIDIA). Without one, speech falls back to the CPU and is much slower.
- About 550 MB for the app, plus about 460 MB for the speech models
- An API key for one provider, or a local model server
- An internet connection on first launch

## First launch

1. Ora downloads the Supertonic voice files (about 380 MB, once).
2. Choose a provider and paste its key. Keys are stored encrypted on your computer.
3. Ora loads Whisper (about 80 MB, downloaded once) and starts the speech engines.

After that it starts in seconds. Click the orb or press Ctrl+Shift+Space and start talking.

## How fast is it?

On a laptop with an Intel integrated GPU, the first spoken word arrives about 2 to 4 seconds after you stop talking. Whisper takes roughly 1 second per phrase with Tiny, 2 with Base (the default) and 6 with Small. A discrete GPU should be quicker.

## Known limitations

- **The installer is not code-signed.** Windows SmartScreen will show "Windows protected your PC". Click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
- **Tested on Windows only.** No other platform has been tried.
- **Provider support was tested against simulated APIs.** OpenAI, Anthropic and Google were checked with mock servers that reproduce their formats and rules, not with live accounts. If a provider rejects a request, Ora shows the error in the chat, and reports are very welcome.
- **The setup program itself has had light testing.** The installed app was tested thoroughly, but the install wizard and uninstaller have not. Uninstalling leaves your data in `%APPDATA%\Ora` (chats, settings and the voice files). Delete that folder to remove it.
- **GPU resets can happen** on integrated graphics. If speech is slow or shows "CPU", use Settings > Advanced > Reset GPU cache.
- **Speakers can cause feedback.** With "Interrupt by speaking" on, Ora may hear its own voice. Use headphones or turn that setting off.

## Privacy

Audio stays on your device. What you say (as text), Ora's replies and tool results go to the provider you chose. Tools that use the internet (web search, Wikipedia, weather) contact their services directly from your computer. Voice and Whisper models are downloaded once from Hugging Face.
